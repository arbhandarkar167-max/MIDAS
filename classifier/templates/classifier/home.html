Clinical Assessment: The ABCDE Rule

Standard dermatological criteria for recognizing suspicious melanocytic lesions

A

Asymmetry

One half of the lesion does not match the other in shape, contour, or structure.

B

Border

The edges are ragged, scalloped, notched, or poorly defined and blurry.

C

Color

Pigmentation is not uniform. Shades of brown, black, white, red, or blue are present.

D

Diameter

The lesion is typically larger than 6 mm (roughly pencil eraser size), though can be smaller.

E

Evolving

The spot is actively changing in size, shape, surface elevation, color, or symptoms.

Common Inquiries

Quick answers regarding the MIDAS classification system

What image formats are recommended?

Dermoscopic images captured via contact or non-contact dermatoscopes yield the highest accuracy. Standard digital clinical macro photographs in JPG or PNG format under 10 MB are also fully supported.

Is patient data confidential?

Yes. MIDAS executes all model inference and data storage securely within the application server. No clinical records or images are transferred to third-party APIs.

Can this replace a skin biopsy?

No. MIDAS is designed as an investigative decision-support and triage tool. Any suspicious, changing, or irregular lesion requires histopathologic confirmation by a licensed medical practitioner.

Which conditions does the model recognize?

The system classifies lesions into 7 diagnostic categories: Melanoma (MEL), Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC), Actinic Keratosis (AKIEC), Melanocytic Nevus (NV), Benign Keratosis (BKL), Dermatofibroma (DF), and Vascular Lesions (VASC).
